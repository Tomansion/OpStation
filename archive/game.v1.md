## Environment

The player operates the door-control system of a large space station.

The station contains:

- **5 hangar doors**, labeled H1–H5, connecting the interior of the station to space or external docking areas.
- A network of hallways connecting:
  - hangars;
  - living quarters;
  - engineering;
  - medical;
  - security;
  - storage;
  - construction areas.

- **10 hallway doors**, labeled D1–D10.
- A control interface showing whether every door is:
  - open;
  - closed;
  - locked;
  - temporarily unavailable.

The player role is limited to **managing access and isolation by operating doors**.

## Player role

The player is the station's **Door Control Operator** during a busy shift.

Different teams and station systems contact the operator throughout the game.

The player's responsibility is to:

- allow people to move through the station;
- keep restricted areas isolated;
- maintain pressure and security;
- remember temporary instructions;
- coordinate several operations happening at the same time;
- react correctly when circumstances change.

The player's actions are simple. The difficulty comes from knowing:

- **which door** to operate;
- **when** to operate it;
- **why** it is currently open or closed;
- **who requested the action**;
- **whether the original instruction is still valid**.

The player can:

- Inspect the current state of the station doors and hallways.
- Read incoming text messages.
- Hear incoming radio / text-to-speech messages.
- Open a hangar door.
- Close a hangar door.
- Open a hallway door.
- Close a hallway door.
- Answer questions from station personnel.
- Request that a message be repeated.

## Communication

Messages arrive through several channels :

- radio calls;
- text messages;
- automated station alerts;

Some messages are short:

> Security Team 2: Open H3. Returning to station.

Others contain several pieces of information:

> Engineering: Reactor maintenance begins at 14:20. Keep D7 closed until we confirm the coolant test is complete. Estimated duration: eight minutes.

Some instructions are later updated:

> Engineering: Coolant test is taking longer than expected. Keep D7 closed for another five minutes.

The player therefore cannot assume that the first instruction they received remains correct.

# Actors

## Security operators

Security teams regularly leave and enter the station.

They may:

- patrol outside the station;
- inspect arriving spacecraft;
- respond to alarms;
- escort visitors;
- investigate suspicious activity.

They frequently request hangar access.

Example:

> Security Team 1: Leaving through H2 in thirty seconds. Open the hangar door.

Later:

> Security Team 1: Patrol complete. Returning through H2.

Security is particularly important during emergency events.

## Construction team

A construction crew is building an extension to the station.

They regularly move:

- workers;
- equipment;
- construction drones;
- materials.

Their work may require certain hallway doors to remain closed to maintain station pressure.

Their schedule frequently changes.

## Maintenance team

Maintenance technicians repair station equipment.

Their activities may require temporary access restrictions.

Examples:

- repairing a hallway;
- servicing a hangar door;
- inspecting ventilation systems;
- replacing damaged electrical equipment.

They may request:

> Keep D4 closed while we work on the power conduit.

Later another technician may request D4 to be opened without knowing that maintenance is still underway.

The player must resolve the conflict.

## Cargo operators

Cargo crews move equipment between storage rooms, hangars and arriving ships.

Their requests are frequent and relatively low priority.

Example:

> Cargo Team: Open D3 and H4 for container transfer.

Cargo requests help create background traffic and interruptions while more important events are progressing.

## Medical team

Medical personnel occasionally transport patients through the station.

Their requests are usually urgent.

Example:

> Medical: Emergency transport from H1 to Medical Bay. Open D2 and D5 immediately.

Medical emergencies may override normal access procedures.

## Citizens and station personnel

Ordinary station residents move around the station.

They create simple, frequent requests.

Examples:

> Resident 431: Waiting at D8. Please open.

> Researcher: Need access to Hangar 5 for shuttle departure.

These requests create routine workload and make it harder to focus exclusively on special events.

## Station Command

Station Command issues high-level instructions.

Examples:

> Command: H1 and H2 must remain closed while the external radiation level is elevated.

Their instructions usually have higher authority than requests from individual actors.

## Automated station systems

The station itself generates information.

Examples:

> Pressure warning: Corridor 7.

> Door H3 obstruction detected.

> External proximity alert.

> Pressure normal.

Automated reports can confirm or contradict information provided by human actors.

# Everyday events

Everyday events are not part of a major storyline.

They create continuous background workload and interruptions.

They should be relatively simple individually, but numerous enough that the player cannot remember everything effortlessly.

## Citizen access

A citizen asks for a hallway door to be opened.

Example:

> Resident 223: Could you open D6? I'm heading toward the observation deck.

The player opens the door.

Later another request may require it to be closed.

## Security patrol

A security team leaves the station.

> Security 2: Open H3 in one minute.

After leaving:

> Security 2: Clear. Close H3.

Several minutes later:

> Security 2: Returning. Open H3.

## Cargo transfer

A cargo team needs a route through several doors.

> Cargo: Container transfer from H4 to Storage. Open D1 and D3.

After the transfer:

> Cargo: Transfer complete. D1 and D3 can be closed.

## Maintenance access

A technician needs temporary access to a service corridor.

> Maintenance: Open D9 for inspection.

Five minutes later:

> Maintenance: Inspection complete. D9 can be closed.

## Shuttle arrival

A civilian shuttle approaches the station.

The player receives several messages over time.

> Docking Control: Shuttle Aster approaching H1.

Later:

> Docking Control: Open H1.

Then:

> Docking Control: Aster secured. Close H1.

## Temporary restriction

A station system creates a temporary restriction.

> Environmental Control: Keep D5 closed for three minutes while we rebalance pressure.

The player must remember when the restriction ends.

## Conflicting request

A person requests access through a door that should currently remain closed.

Example:

> Research Team: Please open D5.

But Environmental Control previously instructed:

> Keep D5 closed until 14:32.

The player must refuse or wait.

This is one of the simplest ways to test whether the player remembers an existing thread.

# Special events

Special events are longer sequences composed of multiple linked messages.

Several special events can progress simultaneously.

Each event should contain:

1. an initial situation;
2. instructions;
3. interruptions;
4. updates;
5. changing conditions;
6. an apparent resolution;
7. optionally, a reopening of the event.

# Special Event 1 — Construction of the station extension

A construction team is building a new module on the exterior of the station.

The event begins calmly and continues for a large portion of the game.

### Beginning

> Construction: Work begins on Extension Module Epsilon at 14:10.

Workers need to leave through H4.

> Construction: Open H4 for crew departure.

After they leave:

> Construction: Crew clear. Close H4.

The construction process requires Corridor D8 to be isolated.

> Engineering: Close D8 before construction begins. It must remain closed while the extension is depressurized.

### Middle

Construction periodically sends status updates.

> Construction: Exterior frame installed.

> Construction: Beginning pressure seal installation.

Some updates have no immediate gameplay consequence. Their purpose is to maintain the narrative thread.

The player receives an initial timer:

> Engineering: D8 can be reopened at approximately 14:24.

Shortly before that time:

> Construction: Delay. Keep D8 closed for another six minutes.

Later:

> Construction: Additional pressure test required. D8 must remain closed.

Meanwhile ordinary citizens may request D8.

> Resident: Can you open D8? I need to reach the observation deck.

The player must remember why it is closed.

### End

Eventually:

> Engineering: Extension pressure test successful. D8 can now be reopened.

The construction team returns.

> Construction: Request H4 open for crew return.

Once everyone is inside:

> Construction: Crew accounted for. Close H4.

The event is marked complete.

### Possible reopening

Several minutes later:

> Environmental Control: Pressure drop detected near Extension Epsilon.

The construction incident becomes active again.

> Engineering: Close D8 immediately.

This tests whether the player recognizes that the new information belongs to an earlier supposedly completed thread.

# Special Event 2 — Damaged transport ship

A cargo ship arrives unexpectedly.

### Beginning

> Docking Control: Transport vessel Meridian requesting emergency docking at H2.

The ship reports minor mechanical damage.

> Meridian: We suffered a navigation malfunction. Request permission to dock.

The player opens H2.

### Development

Security boards the ship.

> Security: Entering Meridian for inspection.

The crew claims that the damage was caused by an asteroid impact.

Later:

> Maintenance: Damage pattern doesn't look consistent with an asteroid strike.

Security asks the player to keep H2 closed.

> Security: Keep H2 closed until our inspection is complete.

### Conflicting information

A Meridian crew member requests departure.

> Meridian Crew: Repairs complete. Open H2.

But Security has not cleared the ship.

The player must remember the earlier restriction.

### Resolution

Eventually:

> Security: Inspection complete. Meridian cleared.

H2 can be opened.

The vessel departs.

### Connection to later events

During the later invasion scenario, the player may discover that Meridian's damage was actually caused by the invaders.

This connects two apparently separate stories.

# Special Event 3 — Pressure leak

A sensor detects abnormal pressure.

### Beginning

> Station System: Pressure instability detected in Corridor 4.

Engineering asks:

> Close D3 and D4 while we investigate.

### Development

A citizen is still inside the isolated area.

> Resident: I'm between D3 and D4. Please open D3!

The player now has conflicting goals.

Engineering clarifies:

> Engineering: D3 can be opened for ten seconds to evacuate the resident. Keep D4 closed.

The player must understand which restriction applies to which door.

### Resolution

> Engineering: Leak repaired. D3 may reopen.

But:

> Engineering: Keep D4 closed until pressure reaches normal levels.

The two doors therefore leave the incident at different times.

This tests whether the player remembers the exact state of a process rather than simply remembering that "the pressure leak is solved."

# Special Event 4 — Missing maintenance worker

A maintenance technician enters a restricted section.

### Beginning

> Maintenance: Technician Ruiz entering service corridor through D10.

The player opens D10.

### Development

Several minutes pass.

Maintenance reports:

> Maintenance Control: We have lost radio contact with Ruiz.

Security begins searching.

> Security: Open D7. Search team moving toward the service corridor.

Later:

> Security: D7 clear. Close it behind us.

The incident temporarily disappears from the message stream.

### Reappearance

Ten minutes later:

> Ruiz: Door Control? I'm trapped near D10.

The player must remember the earlier event.

They may be asked:

> Security: Why was D10 opened earlier?

Correct answer:

> Technician Ruiz entered the service corridor.

This directly tests delayed thread recall.

# Special Event 5 — Medical quarantine

A shuttle arrives carrying a sick passenger.

### Beginning

> Medical: Incoming patient aboard shuttle Nova. Open H1.

After arrival:

> Medical: Close H1. Patient being transferred to Medical Bay.

Medical requests isolation:

> Medical: Close D2 and keep it closed until the examination is complete.

### Development

Several residents ask to pass through D2.

They must be denied.

Later:

> Medical: Initial test negative. D2 may reopen in five minutes.

Then:

> Medical: Correction. Lab result may be contaminated. Keep D2 closed.

The newer instruction supersedes the previous one.

### Resolution

> Medical: Second test negative. Quarantine lifted. D2 may reopen.

This event strongly tests **provenance and updated information**.

The player must understand that the latest medical report overrides the previous one.

# Special Event 6 — Reactor maintenance

Engineering performs scheduled work.

This event is particularly useful for testing reminders.

### Beginning

> Engineering: Reactor coolant maintenance begins at 14:35.

They request:

> Close D6.

Then:

> Engineering: Coolant flush will take approximately six minutes. Reopen D6 once we confirm completion.

Several unrelated events happen during those six minutes.

### Reminder challenge

At 14:41 there is no automatic message.

The player must remember that D6 is waiting for confirmation.

Later:

> Research Team: Can we use D6 now?

The correct answer is not necessarily yes.

The player must realize that engineering has not yet confirmed completion.

Eventually:

> Engineering: Coolant maintenance complete. D6 is safe to reopen.

# Special Event 7 — Invaders attack the station

This is the final major event and combines all three Keeper abilities.

It should begin as another ordinary incident before becoming a station-wide emergency.

## Stage 1 — Strange vessel

A security patrol is outside the station.

> Security Team Alpha: Investigating an unidentified ship near the station.

A few minutes later:

> Alpha: Ship appears abandoned. Hull damage visible.

The initial interpretation is that it suffered an accident.

## Stage 2 — Suspicion

Security sends another message:

> Alpha: Damage appears to have been caused by weapons fire.

Station Command becomes concerned.

> Command: All security patrols return to station.

Different teams are returning through different hangars.

The player must remember which teams are still outside.

## Stage 3 — Lockdown countdown

Security detects hostile movement.

> Alpha: Multiple unknown contacts approaching station.

Command issues an instruction:

> Command: Begin station lockdown in 90 seconds. Allow all external teams to return before sealing hangars.

The player must balance two requirements:

- do not close hangars too soon;
- do not leave them open too long.

Messages arrive rapidly:

> Security Beta: Returning through H3.

> Construction: Two workers still outside near H4.

> Cargo: Requesting H2 open.

The Cargo request should now be refused because it is not essential.

## Stage 4 — Lockdown

Once teams have returned:

> Command: Seal all hangars.

The player closes the required hangar doors.

Hallway isolation instructions follow.

> Security: Close D1, D4 and D7.

But:

> Medical: Keep D4 open. We are transporting an injured security officer.

The player has to process changing priorities.

Shortly afterward:

> Medical: Patient through. D4 can now be closed.

## Stage 5 — Breach

One hangar door is forced open.

> ALERT: H3 forced open.

> Security: Close H3 immediately!

At the same time, enemies enter part of the hallway network.

> Security: Close D5.

Then:

> Security: Open D6 so Team Beta can retreat.

Then:

> Beta: Through D6. Close it!

The pace becomes significantly faster.

## Stage 6 — Multiple simultaneous threads

During the attack, earlier events return.

For example:

> Environmental Control: Pressure falling near Extension Epsilon.

The construction thread has reopened.

At the same time:

> Engineering: Reactor maintenance area must remain isolated.

And:

> Security: Hostiles moving toward D7.

The player must understand that several restrictions on doors now exist for completely different reasons.

## Stage 7 — Counterattack

Security gradually regains control.

> Security: Hangar sector secure.

> Security: Corridor D5 secure. It may reopen.

But other doors remain closed.

The player must not interpret "sector secure" as meaning that all restrictions have ended.

## Stage 8 — End

Eventually:

> Command: External threat neutralized.

Hangars remain closed until all external checks are complete.

Then:

> Command: Lockdown lifted.

The player restores normal station operation.

The game ends after the station has returned to a safe configuration.

# Keeper Challenges

Keeper Challenges occur:

- occasionally during normal play;
- after important events;
- during the end-of-shift debrief.

They test whether the player understands the current history and state of the station.

## Thread Keeper challenges

These questions test whether the player can connect information belonging to the same event.

Examples:

> Why is D8 currently closed?

Possible answers:

- Construction pressure isolation.
- Security lockdown.
- Medical quarantine.
- Maintenance repair.

> Which event caused H2 to be restricted earlier?

> Is the construction incident finished?

> Which door is associated with Technician Ruiz?

> Why was this case reopened?

## Time Keeper challenges

These test chronology and future intentions.

Examples:

> Which door should be reopened next?

> How long has D6 been closed?

> Which team is expected to return through H3?

> Which restriction expires first?

> What action were you asked to perform after the pressure test?

> Was D2 supposed to reopen before or after the medical test?

Some questions should specifically involve delayed intentions:

> Engineering asked you to do something after the coolant test. What was it?

## Provenance Keeper challenges

These test the source and evolution of information.

Examples:

> Who ordered D4 to be closed?

> Who later authorized D4 to reopen?

> Why did the instruction concerning D2 change?

> Which source reported that Meridian's damage was caused by an asteroid?

> Which source contradicted that report?

> Is the current instruction based on Security, Medical, Engineering or Station Command?

Questions can also test superseded information.

> Medical originally said D2 could reopen. Is that instruction still valid?

Correct answer:

> No. A later medical report extended the quarantine.

# Challenge presentation

Challenges should not feel like academic quiz questions.

They can be disguised as requests from other station personnel.

For example:

> **Station Command:** Door Control, why is D8 still locked?

or:

> **Security:** We need D3. Are there any active restrictions on it?

or:

> **Maintenance:** Who asked you to keep D6 closed?

This makes memory checks feel like part of the fiction.

# Door request conflicts

One of the core gameplay situations should be **conflicting requests**.

Example:

Earlier:

> Engineering: Keep D5 closed during pressure testing.

Later:

> Security: Open D5. We need access.

The player should not simply obey the latest request.

They need to understand whether Security's request overrides Engineering's restriction.

This allows the game to test not only memory but the player's understanding of **active constraints**.

# Priority hierarchy

A simple priority system can make conflicts understandable.

For example:

1. Emergency life safety
2. Station Command
3. Security emergency
4. Medical emergency
5. Engineering safety restriction
6. Operational requests
7. Civilian convenience

The hierarchy does not need to be displayed explicitly once the player understands it.

This produces situations such as:

**Engineering:** Keep D2 closed.

then:

**Medical Emergency:** Open D2 immediately for patient transport.

The medical emergency temporarily overrides the engineering restriction.

After the patient passes, however, **the original restriction becomes active again**.

That is a particularly valuable Keeper challenge.

The player must remember:

> Opening the door did not cancel the reason it was supposed to be closed.

# Event states

Each major event should internally have a state such as:

**Not started**

→ **Active**

→ **Waiting**

→ **Active**

→ **Resolved**

→ **Reopened**

→ **Resolved**

This is important because the game should contain many moments where **nothing is currently happening in a thread, but the thread is not finished**.

For example:

Construction:

**ACTIVE** — workers exiting.

Then:

**WAITING** — construction underway.

Then:

**ACTIVE** — workers returning.

Then:

**RESOLVED**.

The distinction between **waiting** and **completed** is one of the major things the game should test.

# Door state versus door reason

Internally, the game should track these separately.

For example:

**D8**

Physical state:

> CLOSED

Reason:

> Construction pressure isolation.

Requested by:

> Engineering.

Since:

> 14:10.

Expected change:

> Reopen after pressure test.

This distinction is central to the experiment.

The game is not primarily testing whether the player knows that **D8 is closed**.

It is testing whether they know:

> **why D8 is closed, since when, based on whose instruction, and what needs to happen before it can reopen.**

# Difficulty progression

## Phase 1 — Learning

Approximately 3–5 minutes.

Only everyday events occur.

Usually:

- one active request at a time;
- short delays;
- simple open/close sequences.

The player learns the interface.

## Phase 2 — Multiple threads

Several special events begin.

Approximately:

- 2–3 concurrent major threads;
- routine interruptions;
- delayed instructions;
- occasional conflicting requests.

## Phase 3 — Memory pressure

Events disappear for longer periods.

A previously active thread may not generate a message for 5–10 minutes.

The player must remember that it still exists.

More updated and superseded instructions appear.

## Phase 4 — High workload

Routine requests become more frequent.

Four or five processes may be active simultaneously.

Messages may arrive while the player is handling another request.

## Phase 5 — Invasion

The final event dramatically increases the pace.

Earlier threads also return.

The player must combine:

- immediate actions;
- current restrictions;
- delayed obligations;
- actor locations;
- previous instructions;
- provenance.

# Failure design

Incorrect actions should not usually cause an immediate game over.

Instead, they should have consequences.

Example:

The player opens D8 despite the construction restriction.

> WARNING: Pressure loss detected.

They must close D8 again.

The mistake is recorded.

Similarly, closing a hangar too early might produce:

> Security Alpha: Door Control! We're still outside!

The player then has to reopen it.

This lets the experiment measure errors without repeatedly interrupting the experience.

# Scoring

The player receives a station performance score.

Possible measures include:

- correct door operations;
- incorrect door operations;
- delayed responses;
- forgotten future actions;
- incorrectly reopened doors;
- doors left open unnecessarily;
- missed restrictions;
- correctly answered Keeper Challenges;
- incorrect provenance answers;
- incorrect thread identification;
- response time;
- number of times information had to be rechecked.

The player-facing score can be simplified into categories such as:

**Station Safety**
**Operational Efficiency**
**Crew Satisfaction**
**Incident Awareness**

The research data can remain much more detailed behind the scenes.

# End-of-shift debrief

After gameplay, the player receives a short command debrief.

Questions cover the entire session.

Examples:

> Which incident caused D8 to remain closed for the longest period?

> Which security team discovered the abandoned vessel?

> Why was the Meridian prevented from departing?

> Which event was reopened after initially being considered resolved?

> What was the original reason D2 was closed?

> Who changed the instruction concerning D2?

> Which action were you supposed to perform after reactor maintenance?

Some questions should refer to events that have not appeared for several minutes.

These provide a direct test of longer-term memory.

# Experimental structure

The same game can be used to compare performance **without the Keeper application** and **with the Keeper application**.

## Condition A — Without Keeper

The player receives:

- messages;
- current door states;
- basic game controls.

They may rely on:

- memory;
- notes;
- physical tokens, if allowed.

## Condition B — With Keeper

The player additionally has access to the application being tested.

The application can help expose:

### Time Keeper

- when something happened;
- how long a restriction has existed;
- upcoming actions;
- reminders.

### Thread Keeper

- messages grouped by incident;
- current event state;
- completed versus waiting threads;
- reopened events.

### Provenance Keeper

- source of each instruction;
- history of changes;
- latest valid information;
- contradictory reports.

The game itself remains mechanically identical.

Only the player's information-management tools change.

# Scenario balancing

The exact same story should not be used twice because the player would remember it.

Instead, create **paired scenarios** with equivalent structures.

For example:

### Scenario A

Construction restriction:

> D8 must remain closed.

### Scenario B

Laboratory containment:

> D3 must remain closed.

Both scenarios contain:

- an initial restriction;
- a promised end time;
- two delays;
- unrelated interruptions;
- an attempted conflicting access request;
- eventual clearance;
- later reopening of the event.

The stories differ, but the cognitive task is equivalent.

This makes comparison between the two conditions much more meaningful.

# Core experience

The game should initially feel easy.

The player receives requests such as:

> Open H2.

> Close D4.

> Security returning through H1.

Over time, the real problem emerges.

The player starts thinking:

> Why is D4 closed?

> Was Security Team Alpha already inside?

> Did Engineering ever clear D8?

> Was that instruction replaced by a newer one?

> I know I was supposed to reopen something after five minutes…

By the final invasion, the challenge is no longer operating doors.

It is maintaining an accurate mental model of the station while the situation continuously changes.

That makes the door-control game a direct but disguised test of the **Time Keeper, Thread Keeper and Provenance Keeper** features.
